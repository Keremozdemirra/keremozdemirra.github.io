Four instruments, and what each of them refuses to do

A valuation that prints how much of itself is the perpetuity behind the forecast. Two market sizings with the gap between them left on the page. A runway that says which month the money runs out and what one more hire costs in weeks. A unit converter that names the missing input rather than guessing it. Every figure is arithmetic on numbers you type. Each instrument saves its state in the address bar, so a result is a link, and each one exports its table.

Cash flows are in one unit of your choosing, the same unit throughout. The share of the enterprise value carried by the terminal value does not depend on which unit that is. There is no net debt, no share count and no per share figure here, because those would turn a decomposition into a price.

    What this page does not know

      Every number on this page is arithmetic on figures you supplied. None of it is a valuation, a market size, a forecast or advice. There are no benchmarks, no industry averages and no default assumptions anywhere in it. Where an example is loaded it is labelled as an example and exists only to show the arithmetic working.
