Tools

Working instruments for the kind of arithmetic that usually wants a spreadsheet, a consultant, or both. They run inside this site, they cost nothing, and they ask for nothing.

How they work

Each one is a page that does a single job and leaves its working on the screen. There is no account to make, no trial, no cookie banner and no analytics, because there is nothing here that watches you. Everything you type is computed in your own browser and never leaves it.

Cost

No sign up, no trial, nothing to buy. A static page costs nothing to serve, so there is no subscription behind any of this and no reason to ever put one there.

Tracking

No analytics, no cookies, no database, no server. Once the fonts have arrived there is no network call left for the page to make.

Where it runs

Your figures stay in the page you typed them into. After it has loaded once, it keeps working without a connection.

Constants

Where a tool holds a rate, a threshold or an emission factor, it prints the publisher, the value and the date that value was read. All of them are collected on one page.

Missing figures

Where a tool cannot know a figure it says which figure is missing and who holds it, and refuses to close the gap with a plausible guess.

The essays

Explorable Economics is not an instrument, and it is kept apart for that reason. You bring nothing to it. The model sitting in the middle of each essay is there so the argument can be checked rather than believed, and every essay ends by saying what its model cannot tell you.

The simulations

SIMEA is my research project, and a written framework is easy to agree with and hard to test. These five agent based models sit beside it so that the claims can be run rather than believed. They are the same kind of page as the essays: nothing to bring, a model in the middle, and a closing section on what the model cannot show.

The SIMEA simulations

Watch a mild preference settle into a severely separated city. Set a recommender loose on a population and run the same population beside it with a neutral feed, with the influence gradient, the autonomy threshold and the entropy of the choice environment defined on screen and computed for the model while it runs. Hold a contagion fixed and change the shape of the network under it. Jam a ring road that has nothing blocking it. Drop grains on a pile until it tells you that the size of the next event cannot be forecast.

The games

Some arguments do not survive being read. You can be told that a short rule beats a clever one when the same two people keep meeting, agree with it, and learn nothing. These put the claim in your hands instead.

Game Theory

Six small painted games, each one a situation people are actually in. A neighbour at a fence every season for forty years. Two hunters who can take a stag together or a hare alone. Ten coins and a stranger who can refuse them. A pasture four households share. A number everybody guesses about everybody else. And a coin nobody can afford to be predictable with.

How they are built

Every page here is a single HTML file. No backend, no database, no account system, no analytics of any kind. Nothing you type is sent anywhere because there is nowhere for it to be sent, and that is also why they can stay free: a static file costs nothing to serve.

The shared design and the shared helpers are spliced into each page when it is built, so what ships is still one file you can save, read or copy whole. The bar at the top of this page and the bar on every instrument are generated from the same few lines, which is why they are the same bar.
