Constants (number theory)

One formula for a whole family of continued fractions

The puzzle

Each of a family of Calabi-Yau equations, known from string theory, produces a never-ending fraction; what number does it approach?

What I found

One formula, with a proof no person has checked yet, covers the whole family; 14 cases also have modular-form formulas, 6 of them unproven.

How long it stood

One member, from a 2024 PNAS table, had been open since 2023; I found no earlier statement of the other fractions or the formula.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23721599
  • arXiv:2203.09426
Details

For a = (a1, a2, 1 − a2, 1 − a1) with a1, a2 between 0 and 1, the exterior square of the hypergeometric Calabi-Yau operator θ⁴ − T(θ + a1)(θ + a2)(θ + 1 − a2)(θ + 1 − a1) is θ⁵ − T m1(θ) + T² m2(θ), and the continued fraction with a(n) = κ m1(n) and b(n) = −κ² n⁵ m2(n−1) converges to 2κ/(T2 − ρT1), where T1 and T2 are Hurwitz zeta values and ρ is a ratio of conifold periods. Row 5 of the PNAS table of Elimelech et al. is a = (1/2, 1/2, 1/2, 1/2) with κ = 8. For the 14 parameter sets whose conifold carries a newform of weight 4 (levels 8 to 864, after Bönisch, Klemm, Scheidegger and Zagier) the record gives the limits in zeta values, Dirichlet L-values and L-values of the newforms, for example 15/(45 ζ(3) − √3 π³) for a = (1/4, 1/3, 2/3, 3/4). Versions 2 and 3 prove these forms for levels 8, 9, 16 and 36 through Ramanujan's theories of signature 2, 3 and 4, and for 27, 32, 108 and 144 through period relations from modular symbols; the other six are numerical identities, checked to at least 656 digits. Both proofs were written with Claude and are checked by exact computer algebra (85 and 49 negative controls) and by AI reviews; no human has checked either. The formula matches the limits to 601 digits in the 14 cases and to about 300 digits for nine other rational parameter sets.

Formulas with proofs for two numbers from a 2024 paper

The puzzle

A 2024 paper in PNAS listed never-ending fractions found by computer and could not give a formula for three of their limits.

What I found

Two of them now have formulas, 1.2042534… and 2.4517460…, the second through a modular form, both with proofs no person has checked yet.

How long it stood

Open since 2023; the paper printed 1.20426 and 2.45174, the first off in its last digit.

Why it matters

The third limit still has no formula; an identity checked to n = 3000 reduces it to the second and one more constant.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23685564
  • arXiv:2308.11829
  • LMFDB 8.4.a.a
Details

Elimelech, David, De la Cruz Mengual, Kalisch, Berndt, Shalyt, Silberstein, Hadad and Kaminer (PNAS 121 (2024) e2321440121, arXiv:2308.11829) give three limits of polynomial continued fractions with b(n) = −64n¹⁰ only as decimals; they matched no combination of zeta values in their PSLQ tests. With a(n) = 8(n⁵ + (n+1)⁵) − 15(n³ + (n+1)³) + 9(2n+1) the limit is 96/(372 ζ(5) − π⁵) = 1.2042534211559321…; with a(n) = 8(n⁵ + (n+1)⁵) − 12(n³ + (n+1)³) + 7(2n+1) it is 8L(f,1)/(186 ζ(5) L(f,1) − π⁴ L(f,2)) = 2.4517460646287829…, where f = η(2τ)⁴η(4τ)⁴ is the newform of weight 4 and level 8 (LMFDB 8.4.a.a). The record proves the first through moments of products of complete elliptic integrals and published evaluations by Zhou and by Wan and Zucker, and the second through periods of the Calabi-Yau operator θ⁴ − t(θ + 1/2)⁴ and identities of Wan and Zucker and of Rogers, Wan and Zucker, who print the one for L(f,1) without a full derivation. Both proofs were written with Claude; exact computer algebra checks their finite identities, three AI sessions reviewed each, and no human has checked them. The third limit, 22.841008…, has no formula; an identity between the convergents of the second and third fractions, checked for n up to 3000, reduces it to the second and one more constant.

Colourings (Ramsey theory)

Ramsey numbers for groups of four

The puzzle

Colour every group of 4 people red or blue; how many people force 5 people with all their groups of 4 red, or 5 with all blue?

What I found

A symmetric colouring shows 48 people are not enough, so the answer is at least 49; with 6 blue instead of 5, at least 159.

How long it stood

35 since 2021 and 67 since 2019; H. Yang raised the 35 to 36 in September 2026.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23689460
  • S. Radziszowski, doi:10.37236/21
  • H. Yang, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22698342
Details

R(5,5;4) ≥ 49 and R(5,6;4) ≥ 159, from colourings of the 4-subsets of the projective line over GF(47) and GF(157) that are constant on the orbits of PSL(2,q), found with a SAT solver. Radziszowski's survey DS1, revision 18 (24 April 2026), lists R(5,5;4) ≥ 35 (G. Exoo, 2021), R(5,6;4) ≥ 67 and R(6,6;4) ≥ 133. H. Yang's Zenodo record of 10 September 2026 gives R(5,5;4) ≥ 36, which with the recurrence R(p,q;4) ≥ 2R(p−1,q;4) − 1 (Song, Ye and Liu, 1995; Theorem 1 of S. C. Liu, arXiv:1906.00132) gives R(5,6;4) ≥ 71 and R(6,6;4) ≥ 141. The same recurrence turns 159 into R(6,6;4) ≥ 317; the record holds neither that 316-point colouring nor a computer check of it. A script in the archive checks every 5-set and 6-set, with planted errors as a control, and separately written checkers agree.

Molecules (chemical graph theory)

A Mostar index of 5

The puzzle

The Mostar index adds up how unevenly the bonds of a molecule's graph split its atoms; which values occur with at most four bonds per atom?

What I found

Two graphs on 13 atoms with index 5, which the problem's authors thought unlikely, so every whole number except 1 now occurs.

How long it stood

Open since 2024, as Problem 9 of Alizadeh and co-authors.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23685991
  • arXiv:2312.13083
Details

Alizadeh, Bašić, Damnjanović, Došlić, Pisanski, Stevanović and Xu (J. Math. Chem. 62 (2024) 1079-1093, arXiv:2312.13083) show that every integer other than 1 and 5 is the Mostar index of a chemical graph, one of maximum degree at most 4, and ask in Problem 9 whether 5 is the only exception above 1. Two non-isomorphic graphs on 13 vertices and 22 edges with degrees 3⁸4⁵ have Mostar index 5, which answers it; a verifier and a second checker written from the definitions agree. Whether 12 vertices suffice is open.

Sorting (comparator networks)

Finding the median with fewer comparisons

The puzzle

Find a list's middle value with a fixed plan of compare-and-swap steps run in rounds; how few steps fit a given number of rounds?

What I found

For lists of 49, 51, 56, 62, 63 and 64 values, plans one to three steps shorter than any listed plan with no more rounds.

How long it stood

Bert Dobbelaere's list of 25 October 2026; three of the six plans by Mason Hemmer, October 2026.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23677065
  • bertdobbelaere.github.io/median_networks
Details

Median selection networks with fewer comparators than every network with at most as many layers in Bert Dobbelaere's list of median selection networks (page of 25 October 2026, read on 26 and 27 October). The table gives n inputs, d layers and the number of comparators. The networks come from a local search at fixed depth, started from listed networks, and from the list's own constructions (removing inputs, adding a central comparator). The archive's script checks each network exactly through the 0-1 principle and is tested against brute force on 201 small networks.

Networks (graph theory)

A smaller counterexample to a 2025 conjecture

The puzzle

A 2025 conjecture claimed an inequality for every network, and its four authors had checked it by computer up to 9 nodes.

What I found

A 14-node network built from the Fano plane breaks it, the smallest counterexample I know of, and so do infinite families of networks.

How long it stood

Published in 2025; W. Jiang disproved it in September 2026 with 16 nodes.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23669060
  • W. Jiang, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22238691
Details

Conjecture 20 of Chen, Guo, Li and Wang (Electron. J. Combin. 32(4) (2025) #P4.37), on the k-th Laplacian eigenvalue of a graph and its complement, was first disproved by W. Jiang (Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22238691) with a 16-vertex counterexample at k = 7. It also fails for the split graph of the Fano plane with n = 14 and k = 6, and for the split graphs of infinitely many symmetric designs, none of them in Jiang's construction. The record holds exact proofs and a verification script.

Counterexamples to a 2026 eigenvalue conjecture

The puzzle

A 2026 conjecture names the one network, for given numbers of nodes and independent cycles, with the largest sum of its two top eigenvalues.

What I found

A five-node bowtie beats the named network, and with three cycles a second network ties it, so it is not the only one.

How long it stood

On arXiv since 2025, published in Discrete Applied Mathematics in 2026.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23685938
  • arXiv:2504.04389
Details

Conjecture 1 of Zhou, Du and He (Discrete Appl. Math. 384 (2026) 139-144, arXiv:2504.04389) says that among connected graphs with n vertices and cyclomatic number c the graph K₁ ∨ (K₁,c ∪ (n−2−c)K₁) is the unique maximiser of q₁ + q₂, the sum of the two largest signless Laplacian eigenvalues. For n = 5 and c = 2 the bowtie K₁ ∨ 2K₂ gives (13 + √17)/2 ≈ 8.5616 against 8.4893; for c = 3 and every n ≥ 5, K₁ ∨ (K₃ ∪ (n−4)K₁) is cospectral with the conjectured graph. Exhaustive search finds no other failure up to 10 vertices, and a script checks everything in exact arithmetic.

Counterexamples to a 2026 bottleneck conjecture

The puzzle

A 2026 conjecture caps the Cheeger constant, a measure of bottlenecks, of every network and its complement, stars excepted.

What I found

The complete network on 5 nodes already breaks it, as do those on 7, 9 and 13 nodes and six other networks.

How long it stood

On arXiv since June 2026.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23685915
  • arXiv:2606.12751
Details

Conjecture 1 of Kempton, Zaitzeff and Muthuprakash (arXiv:2606.12751v1) states max{h(G), h(Ḡ)} ≤ h(K₃ ∨ Eₙ₋₃) for every graph G other than the star, where h is the Cheeger constant. The complete graph Kₙ violates it for n = 5, 7, 9 and 13, for example h(K₅) = 3/4 > 5/7, and for no other n up to 200; a kite on 10 vertices and five graphs on 7 and 9 vertices found by exhaustive search violate it too. A script checks every value in exact rational arithmetic.

Colourings (Ramsey theory)

The puzzle

Colour the numbers 1, 2, 3, … with 7 colours so that no colour holds two numbers and their sum; how far can you go?

What I found

A colouring up to 1698, two numbers further than before, which also raises three related bounds, one of them a Ramsey number.

How long it stood

1696 since 2021 (F. Rowley), after 1680 from 2000.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23670664
Details

Schur number S(7) ≥ 1698, against 1696 (F. Rowley, arXiv:2107.03560, 2021; OEIS A045652), which improved 1680 (Fredricksen and Sweet, 2000). It gives R₇(3) ≥ 1700, where Radziszowski's survey DS1, revision 18, lists 1698. The archive's script checks every pair. With the template inequalities S(n+5) ≥ 380 S(n) + 148 and WS(n+3) ≥ 42 S(n) + 24 of Ageron, Casteras, Pellerin, Portella, Rimmel and Tomasik (arXiv:2112.03175), the 1698 gives S(12) ≥ 645388 and WS(10) ≥ 71340 (previously 644628 and 71256); version 2 of the record adds both colourings and a script that checks them exactly.

Packing coins in a triangle

The puzzle

Fit a given number of equal coins into a right triangle with two equal sides and make the coins as large as possible.

What I found

For ten coin counts between 81 and 286, larger coins than any public record I could find, by at most 0.07 percent.

How long it stood

Eight since Moki&Julio's 2026 set; 81 and 215 since Packomania's table of 2011.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23668593
Details

Packings of equal circles in the isosceles right triangle with legs 1, each radius certified in exact decimal arithmetic and again, by a second program written separately with Claude, in exact rational arithmetic. Each is compared with the larger of Packomania's value and Moki&Julio's (their list of 18 October 2026); Lai, Hao, Yue and Zhou (Computers & Operations Research 181, 2025) cover this triangle only for N = 151 to 200. Version 1 of the record also held packings for N = 164, 182 and 193; that paper had already published larger radii for them, which I found only after publishing, and version 2 drops them.

Networks (graph theory)

Larger symmetric networks

The puzzle

Connect as many computers as possible, each with a fixed number of cables, so any message needs at most two hops, in a symmetric layout.

What I found

For eleven cable counts between 18 and 39, larger symmetric networks than the table lists, such as 468 computers with 27 cables instead of 392.

How long it stood

Table last updated in 2022.

Why it matters

Larger networks without this symmetry are known for all eleven, so these are records for the symmetric kind only.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23668463
Details

Eleven Cayley graphs of diameter 2 whose orders exceed the Cayley-graph table of the degree-diameter problem (combinatoricswiki, page of February 2022); where the table has no entry for a degree, its value at the nearest lower degree counts. Larger graphs without the Cayley property are known for every degree here: the general-graph table lists 307, 338 and 381 for degrees 18 to 20, and Brown's graphs from projective planes, with q² + q + 1 vertices and maximum degree q + 1, are larger at the other eight (from 381 for q = 19 to 1407 for q = 37).

Astronomy (meteor showers)

Six candidate meteor showers

The puzzle

Camera networks record meteor orbits every night; do they hold showers that no official list has named yet?

What I found

Six candidate showers in Global Meteor Network data, each returning in at least three years, none matching a shower in the IAU lists.

How long it stood

Not in the IAU Meteor Data Center lists of 23 October 2026.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23674099
Details

Six clusters in Global Meteor Network orbit data, C1, C2, C3, C4, C7 and C8, with geocentric velocities from 24.2 to 59.0 km/s; none matches a shower in the IAU Meteor Data Center lists of 2026-10-23, and the record compares each with the nearest listed showers. Each returns in at least three separate years at three or more times its local background and was recorded by 97 to 202 stations. Version 2 adds C4, C7 and C8 to version 1's three.

Networks (graph theory)

Corrections to a second 2025 paper

The puzzle

A 2025 paper in Filomat proposed two related conjectures, again inequalities that should hold for every network.

What I found

Both fail as printed, one at the ring of five nodes and one for networks in separate pieces, and a lemma's proof has a gap.

How long it stood

Published in 2025; the main question, for networks in one piece, is still open.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23669770
Details

W.-J. Li and J.-M. Guo, Filomat 39:13 (2025) 4551-4564: Conjecture 1.2 fails for the 5-cycle, an exception lost when it is derived from Conjecture 1.1, and Conjecture 1.1 fails for disconnected graphs such as C5 + K1, because the proof of Lemma 2.1 assumes that every component satisfies the inequality. The record holds exact computations and a corrected Lemma 2.1.

Astronomy (variable stars)

Twelve periodic variable stars

The puzzle

The TESS satellite records the brightness of many stars for weeks; which ones change in a regular rhythm but are in no catalogue?

What I found

Twelve periodic variable stars, most of them pulsating, each seen in two separate TESS data sets, with no variability type in the catalogues checked.

How long it stood

Not in VSX when checked on 27 October 2026.

  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23685844
  • doi:10.5281/zenodo.23685861
Details

Nine stars in an ecliptic TESS field, seen in sectors 44-45 (2021) and 71-72 (2023): four δ Scuti pulsators, a γ Doradus pulsator, a probable γ Doradus pulsator, a stable 0.43-day modulation, a spotted subgiant with a 2.34-day period and a 3.73-day modulation confirmed in ZTF. Three more in sectors 56 and 83: a δ Scuti pulsator, a hybrid γ Doradus and δ Scuti pulsator and an M dwarf rotating in 0.3503 days. None has a variability type in VSX, GCVS, the Gaia DR3 variability tables or the other catalogues the records list.

Method

Searches and verification were carried out with Claude (Anthropic) under my direction.
